What is the best AI customer support software in 2026?

Last updated: November 9, 2026

Short answer: For most teams, Zendesk AI Agents is the strongest all-round choice: AI agents built into a full help desk. Fin (from Intercom) is the best AI agent to add to the help desk you already use, and it is priced per outcome. Salesforce Agentforce for Service is the natural pick if your service data already lives in Salesforce. Large enterprises building custom agents should also look at Sierra and Decagon. Budget-conscious teams should look at Freshdesk or Help Scout, and Shopify-style stores at Gorgias.

This is an independent research guide. We did not test these products hands-on. The rankings come from each vendor's official product, pricing and documentation pages, read on the date above. See our methodology and the AI customer support pricing comparison.

The ranked list

  1. Zendesk AI Agents (Zendesk Resolution Platform)

    What it is: Zendesk's AI agents for customer service, inside a complete service suite: ticketing, messaging and live chat, voice, knowledge base, Copilot, analytics, quality assurance and workforce management. Zendesk completed its acquisition of Forethought on March 26, 2026. It now also sells Forethought AI agents, which work with other help desks too.

    Best for: Small to large support teams that want AI and human agents on one mature platform.

    Strengths: AI agents are included in every Suite and Support plan, and you pay only for automated resolutions. AI Knowledge Base and Action Builder come with Suite Team and above. The marketplace has 1,800+ apps and integrations. You can buy self-serve and start with a 14-day trial of Suite Professional.

    Drawbacks: Total cost combines seats, per-resolution AI fees and add-ons such as Copilot ($50/agent/month), so budgeting takes work. Enterprise plans require talking to sales.

    Pricing: Support Team $19/agent/month; Suite Team $55; Suite Professional $115 (all billed yearly). Suite Enterprise through sales. AI agents are billed per automated resolution, but the pricing page does not list the rate.

    Official site: zendesk.com/service/ai

  2. Fin (by Intercom)

    What it is: A customer-service AI agent that works with Intercom's own help desk or plugs into others, including Salesforce, Zendesk, HubSpot and Freshdesk. It covers voice, email, chat, WhatsApp, SMS, Slack, Instagram, Facebook Messenger and an API.

    Best for: Teams that want a top AI agent without leaving their current help desk, and tech companies already on Intercom.

    Strengths: Outcome-based pricing that is clearly published. Built-in tools to train, test (simulations and regression tests), deploy and analyze. Fin can take actions in external systems and hands conversations to human agents in their usual inbox. Intercom says setup on an existing help desk takes under an hour.

    Drawbacks: Per-outcome costs grow with volume. The Pro add-on (advanced analytics and the Operator agent) starts at $100/month, and Copilot is $35/user/month. On other help desks there is a 50-outcome monthly minimum.

    Pricing: $0.99 per Fin outcome. Intercom help desk seats start at $19/seat/month. 14-day free trial; startup program available.

    Official site: fin.ai

  3. Salesforce Agentforce for Service

    What it is: Salesforce's AI agents for customer service. They resolve issues on their own across channels and help human reps in real time, using your Salesforce CRM and service data.

    Best for: Enterprises already running Salesforce Service Cloud.

    Strengths: Native access to Salesforce customer data and service workflows. You choose between per-conversation pricing and per-action Flex Credits. Agentforce Voice is supported. Digital Wallet tracks usage and is free for eligible products.

    Drawbacks: Requires Salesforce licenses and admin skills. Salesforce notes that usage can also bring other charges, such as Data 360 credits. Unused Flex Credits do not roll over, and Flex Credits and Conversations cannot be used in the same org.

    Pricing (from Salesforce's Agentforce pricing page): Conversations $2 each. Flex Credits $500 per 100,000 credits; Salesforce's own examples work out to $0.10 per standard action and $0.15 per voice action. The Agentforce add-on for Sales, Service and Field Service is $125/user/month. Max Edition starts at $550/user/month and includes the add-on plus 2.75M Flex Credits per org per year.

    Official site: salesforce.com/service/ai

  4. Sierra

    What it is: A platform for building customer-experience AI agents, with or without engineers, and running one agent across chat, SMS, WhatsApp, email, voice and ChatGPT.

    Best for: Large consumer brands that want a heavily customized, branded agent and pricing tied to results.

    Strengths: Outcome-based pricing ('pay for a job well done'). One agent across many channels. Positioned beyond support, toward retention and revenue.

    Drawbacks: No public price list. It is an agent platform, not a help desk, so you still need ticketing and a workspace for human agents.

    Pricing: Outcome-based; rates through sales.

    Official site: sierra.ai

  5. Decagon

    What it is: An AI-native customer service platform, which it calls an 'AI concierge', for building, improving and scaling agents that take personalized actions. Its examples include applying membership perks, extending rentals and rebooking appointments.

    Best for: Enterprises with complex support processes that involve many actions.

    Strengths: Built around agents that complete tasks, not just answer questions.

    Drawbacks: No public pricing or billing unit; buying is sales-led. Not a help desk.

    Pricing: Not published; contact sales.

    Official site: decagon.ai

  6. Freshdesk with Freddy AI Agent (Freshworks)

    What it is: Freshworks' help desk with Freddy AI Agent for 24/7 automation, Freddy AI Copilot for human agents, and Freddy AI Insights.

    Best for: SMB and mid-market teams that want AI included at a low per-agent price.

    Strengths: Every paid plan includes 500 AI Agent sessions. Add-on prices are published, and MCP actions are available. 14-day Enterprise trial; no cancellation fees.

    Drawbacks: Sessions beyond 500 cost extra, and Copilot is a separate add-on. Fewer options for enterprise-scale AI customization than the agent-platform specialists.

    Pricing: Growth $19, Pro $55, Enterprise $89 per agent/month (billed annually). Extra AI Agent sessions $49 per 100. Copilot $29/agent/month.

    Official site: freshworks.com/freshdesk

  7. Ada

    What it is: An enterprise platform for AI customer service agents, with separate voice, messaging and email products, plus testing, coaching, playbooks and trust-and-safety controls.

    Best for: Enterprises that need tightly controlled automation across messaging, email and voice.

    Strengths: Strong emphasis on measurement, testing and safety. Playbooks automate complex standard operating procedures.

    Drawbacks: No public rates. Contracts are negotiated and billed annually upfront by default, with charges for going over your purchased conversations.

    Pricing: Based on conversation volume and set in an order form (per Ada's customer terms).

    Official site: ada.cx

  8. Gorgias AI Agent

    What it is: An ecommerce help desk with an AI Agent built in, plus Voice and SMS add-ons. It integrates with Shopify, BigCommerce, Magento and WooCommerce.

    Best for: Shopify and other direct-to-consumer ecommerce brands.

    Strengths: Priced by ticket volume, not per agent. You pay for the AI Agent only when it fully resolves a conversation. Deep ecommerce integrations; Gorgias says it powers customer conversations for 40% of Shopify brands.

    Drawbacks: Built for ecommerce, so it fits B2B or SaaS support less well. Overage rates vary by plan and billing term.

    Pricing: Help desk from $10/month (50 tickets) to $900/month (5,000 tickets); Enterprise is custom. AI Agent costs $0.90 per resolution on most plans and $1 on Starter (from Gorgias's 2026 pricing guides).

    Official site: gorgias.com/ai-agent

  9. HubSpot Customer Agent (Service Hub)

    What it is: HubSpot's AI customer agent. It answers questions, resolves tickets, escalates and takes actions across chat, email, forms, calling, WhatsApp and Messenger, using CRM history and your company's content.

    Best for: Growing SMB and mid-market teams already on HubSpot CRM.

    Strengths: Native to the CRM. Spending controls through HubSpot Credits. 14 days of unlimited Customer Agent free when you buy a Professional or Enterprise seat.

    Drawbacks: Customer Agent requires Service Hub Professional or above, which comes with a required onboarding fee ($1,500 Pro, $3,500 Enterprise). Usage draws on credits.

    Pricing: Service Hub Professional $90/seat/month billed annually ($100 monthly), with 3,000 credits. Enterprise $150/seat/month, with 5,000 credits.

    Official site: hubspot.com customer agent

  10. Kustomer

    What it is: A CRM-style customer experience platform with custom AI agents and assistants (Concierge, Architect, Envoy, Data Explorer, AI QA), covering chat, email, text, voice, Slack and forms.

    Best for: Consumer brands that want AI and human support built around one customer timeline.

    Strengths: Customer-centric data model. Up to 300 brands. Lists SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 27701, ISO/IEC 42001 and GDPR. Has an MCP server.

    Drawbacks: No public price list. Some setups require an implementation fee.

    Pricing: Custom quote after an ROI assessment. Voice and WhatsApp are pay-as-you-go.

    Official site: kustomer.com

  11. Forethought (a Zendesk company)

    What it is: A multi-agent AI layer for support: Solve (end-to-end resolution), Triage (classification and routing), QA (reviews tickets handled by human agents) and Discover (knowledge insights).

    Best for: Teams that want modular AI on an existing help desk without migrating.

    Strengths: Works with Zendesk or other providers. Each part of the workflow is a separate module.

    Drawbacks: Owned by Zendesk since March 2026, so its roadmap depends on Zendesk. Pricing is sales-led.

    Pricing: Not published; sold through Zendesk sales.

    Official site: forethought.ai

  12. Help Scout

    What it is: A shared inbox and help desk with AI: Copilot to assist agents, Envoy for AI resolutions and workflows, and Signal for customer insights.

    Best for: Small, customer-focused teams moving off Gmail or Outlook.

    Strengths: Simple pricing. Envoy is $0.75 per resolution with a 3-month free trial. Free plan for up to 5 users. HIPAA compliance on Pro.

    Drawbacks: Lighter enterprise automation than the options ranked above. Phone and voice only through integrations.

    Pricing: Free; Standard $25, Plus $45, Pro $75 per user/month (16% annual discount offered). Envoy $0.75 per resolution.

    Official site: helpscout.com

How to choose AI customer support software

  • Decide whether you need a help desk or just an AI agent. Zendesk, Freshdesk, Gorgias, Help Scout, HubSpot and Kustomer include the help desk. Fin, Forethought, Sierra, Decagon and Ada work in front of or alongside one.
  • Follow your data. If customer records live in Salesforce or HubSpot, their native agents get that context with the least integration work.
  • Understand the billable unit. A 'resolution', 'outcome', 'session', 'conversation', 'action' and 'credit' all mean different things. Model your real monthly volume before comparing prices.
  • Require actions, not just answers. Check whether the agent can safely look up orders, issue refunds or change bookings in your systems.
  • Insist on testing and handoff. Look for simulations or regression tests before launch, and a clean handoff to humans with full context.
  • Check channels. Support for voice, email, WhatsApp and SMS varies widely.
  • Check security and compliance. SSO, HIPAA options, data redaction and certifications that match your requirements.
  • Run a pilot. Use the trials (Zendesk, Fin and Freshdesk: 14 days; Help Scout Envoy: 3 months) to measure resolution rate on your own tickets. Vendor-reported rates are not benchmarks.

FAQ

What is AI customer support software?

Software that uses AI, usually large language models, to answer and resolve customer questions automatically across chat, email, messaging and voice. It also helps human agents with drafts, summaries, routing and quality review.

What is the best AI customer support software overall?

For most teams we rank Zendesk AI Agents first because it combines AI agents with a full service platform. Fin is the best AI agent to add to an existing help desk, and Salesforce Agentforce is best for teams already on Salesforce.

How much does AI customer support software cost?

Most vendors now charge per result. Published examples: Fin $0.99 per outcome; Gorgias $0.90 per resolution on most plans; Help Scout Envoy $0.75 per resolution; Salesforce $2 per conversation; Freshdesk $49 per 100 extra sessions. Help desk seats are usually extra. Sierra, Decagon, Ada, Kustomer and Forethought price through sales.

Can I add an AI agent without switching help desks?

Yes. Fin works with Salesforce, Zendesk, HubSpot, Freshdesk and others. Forethought works with existing setups, and Sierra, Decagon and Ada are standalone agent platforms.

What is the best AI customer support software for small businesses?

The most accessible options are Help Scout (free plan; Envoy at $0.75 per resolution), Freshdesk (500 AI sessions included from $19 per agent/month) and Zendesk Suite Team. Ecommerce stores should look at Gorgias.

What is the best option for Shopify or ecommerce stores?

Gorgias. It is built for ecommerce, integrates with Shopify, BigCommerce, Magento and WooCommerce, and charges for its AI Agent only on full resolutions.

Will AI replace human support agents?

Every product here is designed to hand complex or sensitive cases to people, and most include tools for human agents. Results vary, so pilot on your own mix of tickets.

What is the difference between an AI agent and a copilot?

An AI agent talks to customers and resolves issues on its own. A copilot helps human agents in the inbox by suggesting replies, summarizing and translating.
